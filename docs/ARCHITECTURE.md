# Current architecture

This checkout holds the local Dreamlike estate direction. Its uncommitted work is part of the current source; a fresh clone or another worktree does not reproduce it automatically.

## Page and startup

- [index.html](../index.html) owns identity, controls, fallback text, and dialog markup. [styles.css](../styles.css) owns type, spacing, responsive layout, and paper surfaces.
- [src/app.js](../src/app.js) is the lightweight UI entry. [src/main.js](../src/main.js) initializes navigation independently and decides whether to load the deferred scene.
- [src/ui/](../src/ui/) owns hero behavior, the illustrated About estate, and Profile, Experience, and Contact dialogs. Back, Escape, dismissal, focus restoration, and the readable no-JavaScript fallback are product behavior.
- [src/scene-entry.js](../src/scene-entry.js) loads the separate scene bundle. The poster remains the first visual; live rendering and optional models do not block navigation.

The normal authored scene keeps shared terrain, sky, camera, and model activation in index.js. Procedural construction is separated into legacy-world.js and instantiated through deferred-world.js when a comparison, low-quality path, or authored-asset fallback needs it. Keep the legacy placements/materials intact when changing those modules. URL comparison precedence is centralized in scene-modes.js.

Scene subsystems share applyQuality, resize, update, and dispose lifecycle hooks where relevant. Derived resources must restore borrowed model/material state before disposal; quality changes and failed or stale asset loads must preserve a usable fallback.

Adaptive quality changes only per-frame cost: pixel ratio, shadows, post-processing, counts and leaves. index.js passes the startup tier as the `assetTier` quality context, which the model, stone, ground, earth, grass and brick controllers use instead of the profile tier, so a step never blanks or re-downloads models or terrain maps. Frame intervals are sampled only after the scene is revealed, and not for three seconds after a resize, resume, dialog release or asset commit (quality.js `sampleRevealed()` and `holdSampling()`). A revealed scene never steps its visuals down to low: the governor's low step keeps the current profile and lowers only the pixel ratio to 1, the composer resolution every tier had before device-pixel targets, and recovering from it restores the tier's cap. Samples are display intervals, not render cost, so recovery counts only frames that keep up with a 60 Hz or faster display: the window's 10th percentile is at most 17.5 ms and the average within 10% of it, which stays under the 20 ms downgrade line. A capped or GPU-bound steady rate never counts as headroom. A 120 Hz panel held to a GPU-bound 60 fps still looks like a 60 Hz display with headroom; the backoff bounds that case. The wait doubles after each recovery that is followed by another downgrade. After two such oscillations, recovery stops.

runtime.js renders only frames a visitor can see. Before the reveal, the transparent canvas redraws only when a status, model commit, resize or scroll invalidates it; the frame that sees readiness shows the canvas and resumes animation. An open dialog holds the tour at once, and 450ms later, after the dim overlay has faded in, a named scheduler hold stops rendering until the last dialog closes (`createPanelHold()`). A resize draws one frame behind the dialog, then the hold returns. A tour dip in progress when the tour is held shows undimmed. The footer's Pause scene control calls `scene.setVisitorPaused()` (read back with `scene.isVisitorPaused()`): it pauses the tour and, from the reveal onward, holds rendering through a second named hold (`createVisitorHold()`) that composes with the dialog's. A resize, context restore or content change (model, map, shader, font) draws one still frame; scroll does not. The sceneDebug developer camera lifts the hold while it runs and restores the held frame on exit. A stored `scene.visitorPausedPreference` applies at the reveal and keeps the first revealed frame. Without the legacy world, the sun's shadow map redraws only after a shot focus, quality, resize, model or treatment change. The legacy world keeps per-frame shadows for its animated clutter. If the procedural fallback fails to build, the scene stops and leaves the static poster.

rendering.js also checks the underlying WebGL context before entering the composer. This covers physical context loss before the browser's queued loss event reaches Three.js, while preserving the existing restoration and disposal lifecycle.

The composer's targets and passes follow the canvas in device pixels. The pixel ratio is capped at 1.5 on high, 1.25 on balanced and on every touch-primary device, and 1 on low. The renderer's own antialiasing is off because it would reach only the final full-screen pass. On WebGL2 with float color buffers, high draws the scene into one four-sample target, which Three resolves once per frame, and copies it into the composer's read buffer. Bloom and grading then draw into single-sample ping-pong targets, which carry no depth buffer while the scene has its own. A tier change reallocates the scene target. Star sprites and solar prominences are sized in CSS pixels, bloom keeps its CSS-pixel resolution, and the comparison views' ink contour samples one CSS pixel apart, so they keep the look reviewed at DPR 1.

### Start-up cost

The page opens two WebGL contexts: the probe in [shared/webgl-probe.js](../src/shared/webgl-probe.js) and the renderer. The probe caches the texture-size and anisotropy limits that the quality tier uses. index.js passes those limits to quality.js, which opens its own probe context only when they are unknown. All three places request `powerPreference: "default"`, because the ambient scene does not need a discrete GPU.

rendering.js `compileShaders()` links the scene's programs through `renderer.compileAsync` and KHR_parallel_shader_compile, so they do not stall a draw. It compiles against a composer target, because program keys differ for off-screen output. It always settles. It returns false at once when compileAsync or the extension is unavailable, because the draw would link anyway. It also returns false when compileAsync rejects or after two seconds, and the next draw then links whatever is left, as before. index.js runs one warm-up after initialization and one after each model commit, each in a separate task (runtime.js `createShaderWarmup()`). Until the canvas is first shown, nothing draws while a warm-up is pending, and the reveal waits for it. After the reveal, a committed model that replaces nothing visible stays hidden until its programs are ready. A swap for a visible procedural stand-in draws at once, as before. The tree carries two lights and program keys count lights, so every compile briefly shows the subjects still waiting. Shadow-depth and post-processing programs still link on first use.

Model downloads start beside the scene bundle rather than after the scene's first frame. After main.js chooses the live scene and requests the bundle, it selects the startup tier with quality.js from the cached probe limits. It then requests that tier's complete tower and tree at low fetch priority. The build defines the full hashed model manifest in the scene bundle and only each tier's tower and tree URLs in the UI bundle. A hidden page, which draws no frame, makes the request only if it is shown while the bundle still loads; once the scene has initialized, its first frame requests the models itself. The in-flight responses are shared on `site.scene.prefetched`, keyed by URL. architecture-assets.js uses a response only when its URL matches, and at most once. Validation, the byte budget, abort and fallback behave as before. It makes its own request if the early one failed or was unsuccessful. When the live selection starts, the controller aborts the requests it did not take, such as another tier's; teardown, a failed bundle and a failed initialization also abort them. Static-poster paths, the low tier, unknown texture limits and `architecture=` comparisons make no early request.

On high and balanced film paths, initialization fills the procedural ground with only a flat 4×4 preview, because the earth maps replace it, and textures.js paints the full-size canvases in a task of its own right after. The reveal, a legacy fallback that cycles the film earth off and on, and an earth-map fallback therefore show the painted ground while maps load; a fallback before that task paints at once (`ensureProcedural()`). Comparisons, the low tier and `ground=procedural` or `ground=desert` paint in full at start-up, as before.

[perf-marks.js](../src/scene/perf-marks.js) records User Timing entries that Lighthouse and the Performance panel list under `babel:`. The mark is `scene-entry`, the first moment of scene-bundle evaluation. The measures are `scene-eval`, `init`, `first-frame` (the first frame that draws), `glb-parse:<role>`, `assembly:tower`, `assembly:tree`, `shaders:scene`, `shaders:tower`, `shaders:tree` and `ground-paint` (full paints only). `reveal` marks the first frame that shows the canvas.

## Material and environment treatment

architecture.js preserves supplied geometry, UVs and textures while preparing runtime materials. The complete tower has only a supplied color map, so its 0.90 scalar roughness is applied directly rather than remapped toward 1. The tree keeps its normal and metallic/roughness maps, with roughness mapped into 0.84–0.97. Rendering uses existing fill and hemisphere lights for a restrained cool shadow lift; the sun and lantern remain the warm accents. No light or shadow pass is added.

tree-foliage.js weights triangle selection by surface area, then uses deterministic barycentric placement on the outer upper canopy. It has a fixed 80,000-attempt bound and one instanced mesh, capped at 3600 high / 1800 balanced leaves and hidden on low. Leaf geometry remains outside shot fitting, and borrowed source geometry is unchanged. prop-scale.js measures the tree's own transformed geometry for scale and grounding, so changing a child leaf's extent cannot resize or shift the supplied tree and indirectly alter its camera fit. Quality and teardown retain the same ownership contract.

celestial-field.js shares a world-oriented nebula frame and dust function between estate-sky.js and starfield.js. The still nebula is evaluated within the existing sky shell; selected existing stars form a compact cluster and use the same dust field for attenuation. The star limits remain 4200 high / 2600 balanced / 1200 low. atmosphere.js selects three nebula noise octaves on high, two on balanced, and zero on low or inactive film treatment. Star positions return to their baseline distribution when the celestial treatment is disabled. The layer adds no asset request, draw, render pass or light; existing clouds and reduced-motion clocks keep their separate behavior.

The default film ground uses three earth maps (color, normal, roughness) and two grass maps (color, mask), at the existing 1024 high / 512 balanced resolutions. Grass blends in the ground shader. textures.js disables the earlier authored ground color/normal pair on this path; retained comparisons select that pair or procedural ground as appropriate. Failed optional loads and low/static paths retain their existing fallback behavior.

## Camera and composition

The default is a fitted directed camera, opening on **The watch**, then touring seven tower/tree shots at a 20-second dwell. Masonry study is retained for explicit comparison URLs but excluded from the tour. It is not governed by the old fixed orbit-radius guidance. Shot regions, subject bounds, terrain clearance, portrait overrides, and the DOM space occupied by identity/navigation determine the composition. The tower remains the world anchor; measure visual changes from the selected camera and responsive safe area.

Change shot definitions in directed-shots.js, fitting in cinematic.js, and pacing in camera-tour.js. Recheck both desktop and portrait frames, motion extremes, and reduced-motion behavior. [Scene modes](SCENE-MODES.md) describes explicit comparison URLs and the legacy orbit.

## Assets and build

Editable runtime assets live in this checkout's images and fonts folders. The separate workspace Assets root contains original artwork, preparation history, and QA evidence; it is not published wholesale.

[build.mjs](../build.mjs) assembles source into ignored dist output, including separate hashed UI/scene scripts, hashed CSS, posters, and artwork. Edit source files, then build. Keep the internal AGENTS.md and historical docs out of the public payload; public discovery files are separate. [OPERATIONS.md](../OPERATIONS.md) remains the release authority.

[tools/build-output.mjs](../tools/build-output.mjs) stages output safely. [tools/watch.mjs](../tools/watch.mjs) watches published inputs and serializes/coalesces rebuilds. [tools/dev.mjs](../tools/dev.mjs) combines that watcher with an owned local Wrangler process through [tools/owned-process.mjs](../tools/owned-process.mjs). Failed builds retain the last successful output; watch mode retains earlier hashed assets for pages already open. Refresh the browser after a successful rebuild. [tools/og-card.html](../tools/og-card.html) is the unpublished 1200×630 source for the /og.png share card; its header comment holds the headless-screenshot and palette-encode recipe (see [OPERATIONS](../OPERATIONS.md)).
